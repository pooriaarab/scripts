## What this is

One paragraph. What the repo does, in the words a user would use. No roadmap,
no architecture.

## Who it is for

Name them. "Solo founders shipping a first paid product" is a reader. "Everyone"
is not, and a vision that fits everyone rejects nothing.

## What good looks like

Three to five observable outcomes. An outcome is something you could measure or
watch happen, not an adjective.

- A new user publishes their first post in under five minutes.
- A failed publish tells the user which platform refused it and why.

## Explicitly not this

Three to five changes that would be well built, pass every check, and still not
belong here.

**This is the section the review council actually uses.** Every other section
tells a reviewer what to accept; only this one tells it what to reject. Write it
last, and write it from real requests you have already turned down.

- Not a general-purpose scheduler. If it does not touch social publishing, it
  belongs in another repo.
- Not a team collaboration tool. One user, many accounts.

## How it pays for itself

One paragraph. The business model, or the reason the repo exists when there is
none. An internal tool says whose time it saves.

## The current bet

One sentence, with a date. What you are trying to prove right now, and by when.
Update it when the bet changes; a stale bet is how a repo drifts without anyone
deciding to.
